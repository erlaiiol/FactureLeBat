# Phase 1.2-7 — Becoming Our Own PA: Feasibility Research

Standalone research doc (2026-08-25), not part of the docs/1.3/ folder —
this isn't workflow-automation UX, it's a re-examination of Phase 1.2's
own non-goal ("FactureLe will not become a Plateforme Agréée itself," see
`docs/roadmap.md` Phase 1.2's Non-goals). Requested by the user after
asking how the SUPER PDP integration actually works day-to-day for an
auto-entrepreneur — a natural follow-up question: *what would it actually
take to not need a third party at all?* This document answers that with
real numbers, not a re-statement of the original one-line non-goal.

**No code in this phase — pure research and a decision record**, same
shape as Phase 1.2-1's own PA-selection research.

## The question

Immatriculation as a PA/PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire —
the reform's own official term; "PA" is the colloquial/legacy name this
project has used throughout, kept here for consistency) is a real,
state-regulated certification process. What does it concretely require,
and at what point — if any — would it make more sense for FactureLe to
hold that certification itself instead of paying a per-invoice fee to
SUPER PDP?

## What immatriculation actually requires (sourced 2026-08-25)

Per the governing procedure (Décret n° 2022-1299 du 7 octobre 2022) and
current guides on the process:

**Administrative dossier** (filed via demarche.numerique.gouv.fr, ~12
supporting pieces): SIREN, a current tax-compliance attestation, and full
RGPD documentation (treatment registers, sub-processors, retention
periods, technical/organizational safeguards).

**Security certification** — the hard requirement: an **ISO/IEC 27001
certification from an independent certifier**, attesting to a real
information security management system, plus proof that all systems and
data stay within the EU (no third-country transfers). Sensitive hosted
components may additionally need **SecNumCloud** qualification.

**Technical dossier**: a detailed description of the issuance/
transmission/reception pipeline, authentication procedures, secure
data-transmission protocols to the tax administration, and — critically —
**interoperability test reports with another candidate or already-
immatriculated platform** (Peppol has emerged as the de facto default
protocol for this).

**Process**: ~2 months for the administration to review a complete dossier
and grant "immatriculation sous réserve" (provisional); definitive
registration follows only after passing live interoperability tests
against the PPF and other platforms. Registration lasts **3 years**, not
auto-renewed — renewal needs substantially the same dossier resubmitted,
filed at least 5 months before expiry.

**Ongoing obligation, not a one-time cost**: a **conformity audit report
from an independent certifier within 12 months** of registration, and
again for every renewal cycle. Missing it gives a 3-month remediation
window before the registration lapses automatically.

**Cost**: not formally fixed by the regulation itself, but real-world
estimates for the infrastructure, certification, and audit costs involved
run from **several hundred thousand to several million euros** to reach
and maintain immatriculation — this is the number that actually answers
the feasibility question, not the dossier checklist above.

## The economics, concretely

SUPER PDP's own published pricing (already recorded in Phase 1.2-1):
**€0.01/invoice up to 10,000/month, degressive beyond**. At a scale this
app could plausibly reach in the next few years — say 2,000 active
companies each transmitting ~20 FACTUREs/month, 40,000 invoices/month —
that's on the order of a few hundred euros a month in PA fees. Even at
10x that volume, we're talking low thousands of euros a year, not the
hundreds-of-thousands-to-millions a self-hosted PA certification costs
before it transmits a single invoice, plus a recurring ISO 27001 audit
cycle indefinitely after that.

**There is no volume at which self-certifying breaks even against paying
SUPER PDP's per-invoice fee**, for a product at this app's actual and
realistically-projected scale. Becoming a PA only makes sense for a
business where operating the PA *is* the product (SUPER PDP's own
position) or for a company already running e-invoicing infrastructure at
a scale several orders of magnitude past a solo-built SaaS's addressable
market — think a major accounting-software vendor bundling it as one
feature among a much larger platform, amortized across a customer base
this app doesn't have and isn't sized for.

## Decision

**Reconfirms Phase 1.2's original non-goal, now with real figures behind
it rather than a general "out of proportion" judgment call.** Not a
one-way door, though: the honest trigger condition, if this is ever worth
revisiting, is a scale conversation (order-of-magnitude more active paying
companies than today) combined with SUPER PDP's own pricing or reliability
becoming a genuine problem at that scale — neither of which is remotely
close today. Worth a fresh, real check (same "verify the primary source,
don't trust a stale number" discipline as Phase 1.2-1's own PA-landscape
caution) if that combination ever actually shows up, not on a fixed
calendar.

## Non-goals

- No implementation of any kind — this is a reference document for a
  decision already made, revisited with real sourcing.
- No commitment to revisit this on a schedule — see the trigger condition
  above instead of a calendar date, which would just go stale.
